Q3 — Customer Segmentation: Takeaways
========================================

SUMMARY
-------
Neither of the two demographic segments that come with the data — MOSAIC lifestyle group and
DominantBuyingPowerClass — actually predicts how much a household spends at Coop. Buying power is
even counterintuitive: "low buying power" households outspend "very high buying power" households.
The real segmentation signal is plain behavioral concentration: the top 10% of households (by
actual spend) drive nearly half of all profit. MOSAIC group IS useful, but for a different
purpose — it clearly shows what different segments buy, not how much.

(All numbers below computed directly from the raw CSV, independent of the notebook, as a check on
what running it should produce.)


1. DOES MOSAIC GROUP PREDICT VALUE? — BARELY
------------------------------------------------
Compared each segment's share of households against its share of total profit. Every segment
tracks almost exactly — e.g. the biggest group, B METROPOLITISKA PIONJÄRER (Metropolitan
Pioneers), is 21.2% of households and 21.3% of profit.

Avg profit per household across all 14 real segments ranges only from 357 to 433 SEK (2 months) —
a narrow band, not a meaningful split. MOSAIC group does NOT identify Coop's most valuable
customers.


2. DOES BUYING-POWER CLASS PREDICT VALUE? — NO, AND IT'S COUNTERINTUITIVE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Same test for DominantBuyingPowerClass. Range is similarly narrow (363-417 SEK), and the ordering
doesn't even make directional sense:

  LAG_KOPKRAFT (low buying power)              417 SEK avg profit/household
  MYCKET_HOG_KOPKRAFT (very high buying power)  402 SEK avg profit/household

An externally-assigned "purchasing power" label does not predict actual spend at Coop. Worth
stating plainly: this field isn't useful for value-based targeting as-is, and may reflect a
household's overall income rather than how much of that gets spent at Coop specifically (vs.
competitors).


3. WHAT ACTUALLY PREDICTS VALUE: BEHAVIORAL CONCENTRATION
---------------------------------------------------------------
Ranked households by actual 2-month profit and checked concentration (Lorenz-curve style):

  Top 10% of households drive  47.9% of total profit
  Top 20% of households drive  69.0%
  Top 25% of households drive  75.8%
  Top 50% of households drive  94.1%

Half of Coop's customer base contributes almost nothing (5.9% of profit) over this window. A
simple "heavy vs. light shopper" behavioral split captures far more than either demographic label.

Organic share by MOSAIC group — also narrow (~3.9-4.4% among top segments). No segment stands out.
Sustainability purchasing does not look like a strong demographic-segment story in this data.


7. MISSING / UNKNOWN SEGMENT DATA
-------------------------------------
~0.5% of households have no usable MOSAIC label (Okänt / missing), contributing a proportional
~0.5-0.6% of profit. Small enough not to undermine segment-based analysis overall — worth a
one-line caveat, not a major concern.


8. RECOMMENDED ACTIONS FOR COOP
-----------------------------------
1. Stop leading with MOSAIC / buying-power for value-based targeting — neither meaningfully
   separates high- from low-value households. Build a simple behavioral (RFM-style) segment
   instead: the top 10-20% of households by actual spend deserve a dedicated retention/loyalty
   program, since they already drive roughly half of all profit.
2. Use MOSAIC for assortment/marketing, not value targeting — the category-affinity indexing is
   the genuinely useful signal (tobacco/juice for rural traditionalists, flowers/tobacco for
   family-centered followers, beauty/health for high-spending villa households). Real basis for
   localized assortment or targeted promotions.
3. Investigate the top 10% of households directly — since demographic labels don't explain why
   they're so valuable, a follow-up on WHAT they buy and HOW OFTEN would be more useful than
   further demographic slicing.
4. Don't assume buying-power class is a reliable proxy for Coop share-of-wallet — worth
   investigating separately if pricing/promotion decisions currently lean on this field.
